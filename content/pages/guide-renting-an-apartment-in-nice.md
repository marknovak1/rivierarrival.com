sections:
  - heading: "A landlord cannot ask for a random pile of paper"
    body: |-
      This is the part that calms people down. For a private home, the owner or the agency may check who you are, where you live, what you do and what you earn. Service-Public publishes the list of documents they are allowed to ask for, from a decree of 5 November 2015. Asking for something off that list can cost them a fine of up to 3,000 euros, or 15,000 euros if the landlord is a company.

      They also cannot take money to "reserve" the apartment before the lease is signed. At the signing they can take the rent, the deposit and the proper agency fees for the letting. Other sums, even if a clause pretends you agreed, are not allowed.

      You may send copies. They may ask to see the original. Anything you send has to be in French or translated into French, and the amounts have to be converted into euros. A US or Canadian tax notice is useful here: if the income was not taxed in France, the list allows the equivalent document from the tax authority of the country that did tax it.

      The public tool for putting that file in one place is DossierFacile. Service-Public points tenants and landlords to it. I still read the file before it goes out, because a complete upload and a convincing one are not the same thing.

  - heading: "What the file usually contains"
    body: |-
      For you, the future tenant, the allowed pieces group like this.

      Identity, one current document: a French or foreign passport, a national identity card, a driving licence, or a residence card.

      Address, one document: the last three rent receipts, or a letter from the previous landlord saying you are up to date; or a sworn statement from the person housing you; or a tax notice on property, or a title, for your main home.

      Work, one or more: an employment contract or a letter from the employer with the job, the pay, the start date and the trial period if there is one; a student card; or the registration papers if you are self-employed.

      Income, one or more: the last or previous tax notice, including the foreign equivalent; the last three payslips; pension or benefit statements for the last three months; property income. Retirees lean on the pension lines. Employees lean on the payslips.

      A guarantor, the caution, is a person or an organisation that signs to pay the rent, the charges and the tenant's repairs if you do not. A human guarantor has their own closed list: identity, address, work and resources, close to yours but not identical. When the guarantor is Action Logement, you do not invent a new stack of paper. You give the landlord the Visale certificate.

      Agencies will still look at whether the rent sits easily against your income. You will hear a ratio repeated as if it were a statute. It is not in the decree that lists the documents. Treat it as a habit of this market. If a file is being refused for that reason, tell me. Sometimes the apartment is wrong. Sometimes the file is telling the story in the wrong order.

  - heading: "A guarantor, in real life"
    body: |-
      A caution is a signature, not a favour someone mentions on the phone. The act of caution is a written commitment. Landlords on this coast like a guarantor they can picture: someone in France, with French payslips, who looks obvious on paper. That is awkward when your parents are in Toronto or Ohio and your income is a pension paid in dollars.

      You can still offer a person abroad. Their documents have to come in French, with euros on the page. Some agencies will not want them. That refusal is common. It is also why Visale exists, for the people Visale actually covers. It is not a blanket "foreigners get a free guarantor" stamp, and I would rather you knew that before you count on it.

  - heading: "Visale, and who it is actually for"
    body: |-
      Visale is a free guarantee from Action Logement. If you are accepted, they cover the rent up to the ceilings on their site. You apply, you receive a certificate (Visale also calls this a visa, which is confusing, because it is not your immigration visa), and that certificate is what the landlord files.

      The landlord's own conditions, on visale.fr, matter as much as yours. The home has to be your main residence, unless it is a bail mobilité. It is rented empty or furnished, decent, and in France. The lease has to follow the law of 6 July 1989 and include a resolutory clause. It has to be signed after the Visale contract is issued, and before that certificate expires. It cannot sit on top of another guarantee, whether that is a person or an insurance policy. It cannot be with the landlord's family. In a shared flat, each roommate needs their own lease.

      Who can ask:

      - From 18 to 30 inclusive, Visale says it is open whatever your professional situation, as long as the other conditions are met. Minors are not, except a narrow case for someone of at least 17 years and 10 months, and even then the lease waits until they are 18.
      - Over 30, it is much tighter. Visale lists private-sector or agricultural employees in a few situations: within six months of starting (including a trial period, a short contract or temp work), or with net monthly pay of at most 1,710 euros, or within six months of a job transfer, or with a job offer that starts within three months. A confirmed permanent contract outside those cases is not the point of the scheme.
      - Over 30, Visale says a retiree is not eligible unless they also have a qualifying salaried job. Public-sector employees are not eligible. Self-employed people over 30 are not eligible. Someone with no job is not eligible unless they have a private-sector or agricultural job offer starting within three months.

      There is a second gate that catches newcomers. For someone who is not French and not already living in the EU, the EEA, Switzerland, Andorra, Monaco or San Marino, Visale asks for a resident card, or for a visa that counts as permission to work. A visitor visa, the one that forbids work, does not match that sentence. A non-EU student needs a student long-stay visa or a student residence card, and the dates have to have started.

      The rent Visale will cover is capped at 50 percent of the income it agrees to count, and then capped again by where the apartment is. On the pages I read, the ceilings for charges-included rent were up to 1,940 euros in Île-de-France, up to 1,575 euros in agglomerations of more than 100,000 people (and in Corsica and the overseas departments), and up to 1,365 euros elsewhere. Students can be covered without income, inside a lower flat rate: 1,000, 840 or 680 euros in those same three bands. Investment income, dividends and rent you receive are on Visale's list of resources it does not count. Use the simulator on visale.fr for the town, rather than assuming Nice, Antibes and Menton all fall in the same band.

      Since 6 January 2026, Visale says the guarantee covers the first three years of occupancy. Contracts signed before that date keep their old terms. At the end of the three years you can apply again if you are still eligible. The salary cap for employees over 30 moved, on that same date, from 1,500 euros net to 1,710.

      So if you are retiring onto a visitor card, I would not build the search around Visale. A human guarantor, or a landlord who will read a pension file properly, is the more honest plan. If you are under 30, or you are starting a private-sector job here and your immigration status lets you work, Visale is worth applying for before you sign anything.

  - heading: "The lease and the deposit, so the numbers have a ceiling"
    body: |-
      I am talking about a home you live in, not a holiday let. Those are different contracts.

      An unfurnished lease runs for three years when the landlord is a private person, and six years when the landlord is a company. It can be shorter, but not under a year, only if the owner has to take the home back for a family or professional reason and the lease says so.

      A furnished lease used as your main home runs for one year and renews if nobody gives notice. If it is rented to a student for nine months, it ends on that date and does not renew by itself.

      The deposit, which people here sometimes confuse with the guarantor, is capped. For an unfurnished home it cannot be more than one month of rent, not counting charges. For a furnished home it cannot be more than two months of rent, not counting charges. It is paid when you sign, it has to be written into the lease, and it cannot be raised later. If the inventory when you leave matches the one from the day you got the keys, Service-Public says the deposit comes back within one month of the key handover. If it does not match, the deadline is different, and it is on that same page.

      Furnished or not also changes the inventory. A furnished let comes with a list of the furniture. Read it in the apartment, not in the hallway on the way out.

faqs:
  - question: "What documents do landlords in Nice ask a foreigner for?"
    answer: |-
      Only the ones on the Service-Public list: identity (a foreign passport counts), something showing where you live now, your work or student status, and your income. If the income was taxed outside France, the equivalent foreign tax notice is allowed. Copies must be in French or translated, with amounts in euros. They are not allowed to demand a document off that list.
  - question: "Do I need a French guarantor to rent in Nice?"
    answer: |-
      The law does not say the guarantor has to be French. In practice many agencies want a person they can read easily, often someone with French payslips. A guarantor abroad can be offered if the papers are translated and converted into euros. Visale can replace a person, but only if you meet Visale's own rules, and it cannot be stacked on top of another guarantor.
  - question: "Does Visale work if I am retired or on a visitor visa?"
    answer: |-
      Usually no. Visale says retirees over 30 are not eligible unless they also have a qualifying salaried job, and non-EU applicants need a residence card or a visa that authorises work. A visitor visa forbids work. If that is your status, plan on a human guarantor or a landlord willing to read a pension file.
