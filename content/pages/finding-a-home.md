sections:
  - heading: "Why let me do this"
    body: |-
      The honest truth is that most of what makes a search work here has very little to do with a screen and everything to do with being a real person, in this place, right now. The places worth having often move by phone call, in French, before a listing ever updates. A viewing gets offered for that same afternoon, not next week when you could fly over for it. And knowing that a street looks lovely in photos but is loud until 2am on a Friday isn't something you can filter for.

      I live here. I speak the language every day, not just enough to get by. I can be at a viewing within the hour, and I can usually tell the difference between an owner who's genuinely flexible and one who's just testing the market. None of that is a special talent — it's simply being present, which is the one thing that's hardest to be when you're still packing boxes somewhere else.
  - heading: "Who I do this for"
    body: |-
      This tends to fit people arriving from abroad against a real deadline — a job start date, a school term, a lease ending somewhere else — who don't have a spare month to spend scrolling listings in a language they're still learning. If that's roughly your situation, I'd genuinely like to hear from you.

      It's probably not the right fit if you're in no particular hurry, you enjoy the hunt itself, or you already speak fluent French and know this coast well. In that case you likely don't need me nearly as much as you think, and I'd rather say so plainly than take on a search that isn't really mine to do.
  - heading: "Rentals or a purchase"
    body: |-
      I help with both — long-term rentals for people settling in for a season or several years, and purchases for those who've already decided the Riviera is home for good. The conversation looks different depending on which one we're having, but the underlying job doesn't change: understanding what you actually need, and not wasting your time on what you don't. Tell me which one you're thinking about and I'll meet you where you are, whether that's a first rental while you find your feet or the place you intend to stay in for good.
  - heading: "What it feels like"
    body: |-
      The version I like best is the one where someone lands, is handed a set of keys, and walks straight into a place that's already theirs — instead of dragging a suitcase between a hotel room and a laptop full of open tabs, watching the nightly rate climb while nothing gets decided. I lived the second version myself, in my first year here, and it's exhausting in a way that has nothing to do with the apartment itself and everything to do with not having anywhere to put your bags down. That's really what this is about: getting you to the part where you stop searching and start living.
