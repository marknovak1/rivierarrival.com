sections:
  - heading: "There is no visa called retirement"
    body: |-
      France does not issue a card that says "retiree" for non-European citizens. Service-Public describes a temporary visitor residence card for a foreigner who wants to stay more than three months without working. It is valid for one year and it can be renewed. Religious workers use the same category. So do a lot of people who are simply done with an office.

      If you are a citizen of the EU, the EEA or Switzerland, this card is not yours. You do not need a residence card at all, though you can ask for a European citizen card as a retiree or inactive person if you want one. Algerian citizens have a different one-year visitor certificate. If you are American or Canadian, you are in the "everyone else" case on that page.

      The wider map, including the short stay you can take before you commit, is in [Moving to the French Riviera from the US and Canada](guide-moving-to-the-french-riviera.html).

  - heading: "Three conditions, and a number Service-Public actually publishes"
    body: |-
      Service-Public lists three conditions for the visitor card:

      - Resources that are enough for the whole stay
      - Health insurance that covers the whole stay
      - No work in France, whether as an employee, a shopkeeper, an auto-entrepreneur, an artisan or a liberal profession

      For one person, the page sets the floor at 1,477.93 euros net a month, across a year. The file it asks for is proof of resources reaching 17,735.19 euros for the year. That can be a pension statement, rental income, bank letters, or support from a family member who can show they are able to pay. Family allowances are not counted, because they exist for the children. The prefecture also looks at your housing when it judges whether the money is enough.

      I am quoting the page that was checked on 1 June 2026. Do not memorise the cents. Open the page again when you prepare the file.

      The insurance is private cover you arrange for the length of the stay. It is part of the application, not something you pick up after the card arrives. What French public healthcare looks like once you are actually in the system is a later conversation, and I have written the practical version in [Healthcare](guide-healthcare.html). I will not guess on this page which month a visitor becomes eligible for it. That rule has its own page, and your insurer and the health desk are the ones who apply it to you.

  - heading: "Apply before you move, then keep the card alive"
    body: |-
      You ask for the long-stay visa while you still live in the US or Canada, through France-Visas, and you attend the appointment they give you. The card itself is what you renew in France.

      Service-Public says you file the visitor card online, at the earliest four months and at the latest two months before your current visa or card runs out. The portal is the étrangers en France site. If the prefecture says yes, you collect the card at the prefecture for your address. Around here that is Alpes-Maritimes. If nobody answers within four months, the page treats the silence as a refusal.

      The same page prices the card at 350 euros, made of a 300 euro tax and a 50 euro stamp, paid with fiscal stamps. A late filing can cost more, with a narrow exception if you still hold a valid visa or you are in a case of force majeure. Budget for the 350 euros, and read the late-fee lines before you miss a window.

      If the visa in your passport is a VLS-TS, you still validate it online within three months of arrival. If it tells you to request a residence card, the prefecture step is within two months. Both clocks are in the [France-Visas questions](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/faq) and, in plainer language, in [Visas & paperwork](guide-visas.html).

      Renewal is the same idea, on the same portal, in that same four-to-two-month window, with the resources, the promise not to work, and the insurance shown again.

  - heading: "Where retirees actually sit on this coast"
    body: |-
      Menton is the town people mean when they say they want quiet and a winter that is still mild. Nice is the town they mean when they want a hospital, a train station and a city they can walk without a car. Villefranche is small and beautiful and not always practical. I have written the towns, without pretending one of them is right for everyone, on [Neighborhoods](neighborhoods.html).

      Renting as someone who cannot show a French salary is a specific kind of file. The documents a landlord may ask for, and why Visale often does not fit a retiree, are in [Renting an apartment in Nice as a foreigner](guide-renting-an-apartment-in-nice.html). If you want me to do the viewings, start with [I'll find your place](finding-a-home.html).

      I am not publishing a retirement budget yet. The town-by-town figures are waiting on [cost of living](guide-cost-of-living.html), in blanks, until I have checked them. Please don't take a number from a forum and call it mine.

  - heading: "The tax conversation is not the visa conversation"
    body: |-
      I am not a tax adviser, and a page on a relocation site should not pretend to be one. Being allowed to live here and being tax resident here are different tests. If you are American, ask someone who files on both sides what you still have to send to the IRS after you move. If you are Canadian, ask the same kind of person about leaving Canada. France has its own rules for when your home, your income or your time here make you resident for tax. Get that advice before you move a pension or sell a house. I can help you land. I can't tell you what you owe.

faqs:
  - question: "Is there a retirement visa for the French Riviera?"
    answer: |-
      Not under that name, for a non-European citizen. The usual route if you will not work is the one-year visitor residence card, which can be renewed. You request the long-stay visa before you move, then you deal with the card in France.
  - question: "Can I work a little if I retire to the French Riviera on a visitor card?"
    answer: |-
      No. Service-Public says the visitor card does not allow work as an employee, a trader, an auto-entrepreneur, an artisan or a liberal profession. You also sign a statement that you will not work. If you need to work, you need a different visa from the start.
  - question: "How much income do I need to show to retire to France?"
    answer: |-
      On the Service-Public visitor-card page checked on 1 June 2026, one person must show at least 1,477.93 euros net a month, and the file asks for 17,735.19 euros over the year. Pensions, property income, bank letters and a solvent family member can be part of that proof. Check the page again before you apply, because the floor changes.
  - question: "Do I need health insurance before I arrive?"
    answer: |-
      Yes. Insurance that covers the length of the stay is one of the three conditions of the visitor card. It is not the same thing as joining the French health system later. I would land with the policy already in the file.
