I brought him a knife I'd been ruining bread with since April. He looked at it the way a doctor looks at a patient who waited too long.

Libération on a Friday is already a full conversation — tomatoes, gossip, someone arguing about parking — and then you hear the stone. It is not loud. You follow it.

If you go: arrive before nine if you want him without a queue. He takes cash. He will tell you, kindly, if the knife is not worth saving. Mine was. It now cuts tomatoes instead of crushing them.

I still shop the rest of the market after. That is the other half of the lesson. The sharpener is a reason to go; the rest of the stalls are why you stay.
